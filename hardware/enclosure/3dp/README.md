# CANary — 3D-printable enclosure

Generated by [`hardware/enclosure/gen_case_3dp.py`](../gen_case_3dp.py) `--tube`. Parametric
and geometry-validated: every dimension is derived from the placed board and from Neutrik
drawing 20003890 (`hardware/lib/ne8fbh.pdf`), and the script refuses to write anything that
fails its checks. The outputs are in [`../3dp-tube/`](../3dp-tube/) (STEP + STL), oriented
for printing in [`../bambu/print-tube/`](../bambu/print-tube/), and sliced for a Bambu Lab
H2C as the `.3mf` plates in [`../`](../).

This is the buildable form of the D1/D2 concepts in [`docs/ENCLOSURE.md`](../../../docs/ENCLOSURE.md):
a **one-piece tube** — top, bottom and both flanks printed as one part standing on its rear
end — with two flush end plates screwed to it. (Earlier two-shell "lozenge" and flat-section
variants of the same enclosure were built first, and one was printed as a first article; the
generator can still produce them, but they are superseded and their outputs are not included
in the public repo.)

```bash
python3 -m venv .venv-cad && .venv-cad/bin/pip install cadquery
.venv-cad/bin/python hardware/enclosure/gen_case_3dp.py --tube            # -> 3dp-tube/
.venv-cad/bin/python hardware/enclosure/bambu/orient_for_print.py --tube  # -> bambu/print-tube/
hardware/enclosure/bambu/build.sh absgf-0.4-tube                          # -> canary-case-absgf-0.4-tube.3mf
```

## The parts

| Part | Carries |
|---|---|
| `canary_tube` | the whole body: the superellipse shell, the PCB channel rails, two full-length screw bosses under the top wall, LED window, two service pinholes with guide tubes, the SW201 slot |
| `canary_end_front` | 3 × etherCON NE8FBH; a flush plate screwed to the front end face |
| `canary_end_rear` | USB-C service port — the plate steps in 6.2 mm to meet the receptacle |
| `canary_led_insert` | translucent PLA tile for the LED rebate, with four light pillars down to the LEDs |
| `canary_switch_cover` | PLA cap over the two service pinholes and the SW201 slot: two Ø3.9 pegs push into the pinholes, a key drops into the slot, and it pulls off by hand |

Stood on its rear end face, every layer of the tube is the same 2.5 mm superellipse ring:
**nothing on the shell overhangs**, so the body prints in one material on one nozzle with
no support at all. The guide tubes are the one feature that would hang in the air, and they
carry their own fins (below).

### Superellipse, not ellipse

The cross-section is `|x/66|⁴ + |z/20|⁴ = 1`. A true ellipse does not work here. The
section has to contain both the board's right edge at (56, 0) *and* the top outer corner
of the etherCON cluster at (40.5, 22.9) — three 25.6 mm-tall front plates spread over
81 mm. An ellipse through both of those needs a **70 mm** internal height, because it
throws away all four corners of the space. `n = 4` holds the same soft profile in 40 mm.

It is also flat enough not to roll: over the middle ±30 mm the underside drops 0.2 mm.

### The extrusion axis runs the short way

The three etherCONs exit one **long** board edge and the USB-C the opposite long edge, so
the two flat end faces are 81 mm apart and the case is wider than it is deep. That is
what the board dictates; there is no orientation in which the I/O lands on the 100 mm ends.

### The board sits off-centre, deliberately

`docs/ENCLOSURE.md` established that the etherCON cluster centre is 6 mm left of the board
centre. The board is therefore mounted with **22 mm clear on the left and 10 mm on the
right**, which puts the cluster exactly on the case centreline. The generator asserts this
relationship rather than trusting the two gap figures to stay consistent.

The 22 mm left strip is not waste: it carries the LED window and the left screw boss.

### …and off-centre the other way too

The board is also **3.25 mm forward of the case's Y centre**, and this one is not a
choice. `U301` is an ESP32-S3-WROOM-1 whose 25.5 mm body is centred 7.5 mm in from the
board's rear edge, so the module — antenna end first, as an antenna should be — **hangs
5.25 mm past the edge of the PCB**. The first article proved it: the case gave it 0.5 mm.

The front clearance cannot grow, because the three etherCONs bolt to the front end plate
and that fixes where the board's front edge is. So the whole 6.5 mm of extra depth goes
*behind* the board: `REAR_CLEAR` is 7.0 mm (5.25 overhang + 1.75 air), `FRONT_CLEAR` stays
0.5, and the channel moves forward with the board. The generator checks the module against
the rear face and the section, not just the board outline.

## How the board is held

**The board has no mounting holes.** Its primary fixing is the **six M3 panel screws** that
bolt its three NE8FBH bodies to the front end plate. The channel supports and guides it.

**The board slides in from the front, rear edge first.** The grooves are closed on both
sides and engage 2.5 mm each, with 1.0 mm of side play between the roots; the rails run the
full length of the tube. The left lip stays clear of D301–D304 (an `LED_0603` body spans
board x 3.15–3.95).

**The end plates are screwed on.** Each is the full outer outline, sits flush on its end
face, and takes **four M3 × 8 button-head screws** into heat-set inserts. Two **full-length
Ø9 / Ø7 bosses** run under the top wall; the lower screws go into the channel rails, which
stand 3 mm taller (to z −5.4) so a Ø3.7 pilot has 3 mm of rail around it. Standing up,
bosses and rails are vertical columns — nothing to support. Because the plates are screwed
rather than captured in a rebate, a yanked etherCON cable cannot drag the front plate, its
connectors and the board out of the case.

Sliding the board in, its rear part passes under the guide tubes at z ≥ −2.4 with the ESP32
can at −7.3 and the switches at −5.4, and the etherCON plates only ever enter the last 8 mm
of the tube.

## UI apertures

The board sits **30.4 mm** below the top face, because the etherCON sets the height and the
UI parts are 0603s and 5 mm tacts. Consequences, all handled:

* **LEDs `D301`–`D304`** — a 10 × 22 mm window (x −47…−37; the LEDs are at −40.5) with a **flat-floored** 1.2 mm rebate (the
  top face is curved, so a pocket that followed the surface would rock a flat insert).
  Fit the **printed PLA insert** (`canary_led_insert`: a 12.7 × 25 × 1.2 mm tile with four 4 × 3 mm light pillars reaching to 1 mm above the LEDs), which replaced the 13 × 25 × 1.2 mm smoked acrylic strip on 2026-09-21. Unlike the acrylic top plate in
  `docs/ENCLOSURE.md`, a printed body is opaque, so this window is load-bearing, not
  decoration.
* **`SW301` (EN/reset) and `SW302` (BOOT)** — from the pin map in `hardware/mcu.kicad_sch`,
  both are *service* controls, not user buttons. They get Ø4.0 tool pinholes with an
  internal guide tube reaching to 8.0 mm above the switches, so a stiff wire finds the
  actuator. Printed standing up, each tube would be a 22 mm horizontal cantilever off a
  vertical wall, so each has a **45° teardrop section** (point toward the plate) and a
  **1.2 mm fin under the apex** that drops away at 45° back to the wall. The two tubes are
  10 mm apart and print as one hanging block; the generator asserts the fin's clearance to
  the LED window (0.4 mm), the SW201 slot and the rear face. No 26 mm plungers, which is what a finger-operated button would have needed.
* **`SW201`** (CAN terminator) — a 13 × 5.5 mm screwdriver slot. `LAYOUT.md` rule 6 wants
  it field-reachable; at this depth "reachable" means with a small blade, not a fingernail.
* **Switch cover** (`canary_switch_cover`) — one cap over all three, in the LED insert's
  white PLA, sitting **flush** in a 1.2 mm pocket in the top wall (since 2026-09-27; the
  first version sat on top of the lid). The lid falls 0.7 mm across the cover's 17 mm but
  stays within 0.13 mm of its own chord there, so the pocket floor and the cover's top are
  one plane tilted **2.6°** to follow it: the cover is a flat 17 × 36.2 × 1.2 mm slab, 0.15 mm
  clear all round, whose top ends within **0.08 mm** of the curve anywhere (`validate()`
  holds it under 0.1), and it still prints flat, top face down, pegs up
  (`orient_for_print.py` levels it on its largest face). **1.24 mm of wall** is left under
  the pocket at its thinnest. Two Ø3.9 × 4 mm pegs push into the Ø4.0 pinholes and hold it;
  a key 0.15 mm/side under the slot drops 1.0 mm in and locates it. Past the pocket's front
  end (toward the etherCONs) a **6 × 2.5 mm lever notch** runs down to the pocket floor, and
  the cover's front edge has a **0.6 mm 45° bevel** underneath: a small flat screwdriver in
  the notch, tip under the bevel, levers it out. The built-solid checks probe the pocket
  open and floored at four corners, the notch open and walled, and the bevel present; each
  was shown to fail with its feature deleted. The push fit is unprinted — if it falls out,
  the pegs are `SW_COVER_PEG_D`.

## Lettering

Added 2026-09-24. All of it is **engraved 0.6 mm**, never embossed:
nothing may stand proud of a face that prints on the plate (the rear plate prints outer
face down), and a recess needs no clearance from anything.

| Face | What | Where |
|---|---|---|
| lid | the Stoatworks Labs mark, **STOATWORKS / LABS** (Montserrat ExtraBold, 4.5 mm caps), **CANary** (Montserrat Bold, 7 mm caps) | stacked, 53 × 65 mm, in the clear band right of the LED window / pinholes / SW201 slot; reads with the etherCONs toward you |
| front plate | **CAN 1**, **CAN 2**, **ETH** (5 mm caps) | under `J201`, `J202`, `J401`, centred z −15.5 -- clear of each flange (bottom at z −10.4) |
| rear plate | **USB** (5 mm caps) | above the USB-C well's lead-in |

The lid engraving follows the curve: it is the band between the outer section and the
section 0.6 mm inside it, so the depth is the same across the section's crown.

Source files live in [`../lettering/`](../lettering/):

* `Montserrat-Bold.ttf`, `Montserrat-ExtraBold.ttf` (OFL, `OFL.txt`) -- static cuts of the
  brand's variable font, made by `cut_fonts.py` **with overlaps removed**. A variable
  font's glyphs are overlapping contours (the A's crossbar crosses its legs), which OCC
  builds into self-intersecting faces and invalid solids; the first lettered front plate
  lost 11 cm³ to 35 mm³ of text. Needs `skia-pathops` in the CAD venv.
* `stoatworks-mark.geojson` -- the website's 778 × 602 brand master
  (`stoatworks-website/brand/mark-navy.png`) traced by `trace_mark.py` (potrace). The ink
  -- bow-tie plus the stoat's outline -- is grown 1 px first, so the outline engraves at
  ~0.7 mm at the 44 mm mark width, not the 0.55 mm drawn. The generator reads only the
  GeoJSON, never the master.

`apply_lettering()` runs after every other probe, so those checks all see the plain parts.
It refuses to write unless each engraving lies wholly in material (so no label can run
into an opening or off a plate), each part loses exactly that volume **measured locally**
(OCC's whole-part volume on these spline sections wanders by hundreds of mm³ between
booleans, which would swamp a 24 mm³ label), keeps its bounding box, and is still one
valid solid. The cutters run 1 mm out past each surface rather than stopping on it --
the same coincident-face trap as the screw bosses.

**Not yet printed.** Things to look at on the first one: whether 0.6 mm reads well enough
in orange ABS-GF (a contrasting paint-fill is the usual fix, and 0.6 mm holds one), whether
the stoat's interior lines survive on the tube's lid -- a *vertical* wall when the tube
stands up, so its detail is resolved by the layer height (0.24) along one axis -- and
whether **USB**, engraved into the rear plate's bed face, bridges cleanly.

## Handedness — the export is a mirror image of the model

KiCad's board frame is **left-handed**: X to the right, Y *down* the screen, component
side toward the viewer. This generator works in that frame (case Y toward the etherCON
edge, Z toward the components), and a right-handed CAD kernel reads those numbers as the
**mirror image of the physical board**. The first three articles were exported that way,
and nobody could tell until the rear plate grew a feature that had to meet a connector:
with the board in its channel, etherCONs forward and components up, the USB-C sits on the
*opposite* side to its aperture — and the LED window, the two pinholes and the SW201 slot
are over the wrong side of the board too. The etherCON cluster is 6 mm off the board's
centre, so a mirrored case also puts the three bores 12 mm off the connectors.

Since 2026-09-21 every part is **mirrored about YZ on export** (`MIRROR` in the
generator), after validation and the built-solid probes, which are all distances and do
not care. `--as-kicad` writes the unmirrored solids for comparison with the earlier prints.
The etherCON flange diagonal (`ETHERCON_DIAG`) was derived in the board frame from the
drawing's front view and mirrors with everything else. **Confirmed on 2026-09-21:** the
first mirrored front plate went onto a unit's three connectors with all six panel screws,
outer face out — so both the mirror and the diagonal are right.

## USB-C

The receptacle mouth sits flush on the board's rear edge — and the rear end plate is
**7.0 mm behind that edge**, because the ESP32 antenna on the same edge needs the room (see
above). A plug cannot reach across that: its shell is only ~6.5 mm long, and the plain
13 × 8 mm aperture the plate carried until 2026-09-21 left the port to slim cables, and
then only just — a first article showed it. So the rear plate now **steps in**: a
17.5 × 12.5 mm well stands 6.2 mm off its inner face and stops 0.8 mm short of the board
edge, with a **14 × 9 mm plug aperture in its floor** and a 45° funnel from the well down
to that aperture. A spec-sized moulding (12.35 × 6.5 mm max) passes the floor and seats
fully; a chunkier one stops on the funnel 2.4 mm from the mouth, which is the loss any 2 mm
panel costs. A 1 mm lead-in chamfer at the outer face helps a plug find a port that is 7 mm
down a hole. The well lives on the plate alone — the shell still encloses the whole board
and the antenna keeps its 1.75 mm — and clears the right rail by 1.15 mm. `validate()` checks all of that; `verify_rear_well()` probes the
built solid for a wall, a hollow, a floor and an open aperture. The aperture is centred on
the real receptacle shell (8.94 × 3.26 mm on the board's top surface, z −8.77), not on the
inflated 9.7 × 3.8 stand-in the old aperture used.

The well is also why the rear plate prints **outer face down**: the well stands up off the
plate and the funnel carries the floor at 45°, so nothing on it overhangs and the whole face
is on the bed. `build.sh absgf-0.4-tube rearplate` slices it alone
(`../canary-rearplate-absgf-0.4-tube.3mf`).

This supersedes the outside-relieved wall proposed as the answer to `docs/ENCLOSURE.md`
open item 6, which only made sense with the end plate against the board edge.

| 1 | `canary_switch_cover` in the same PLA |

### Insert OD, and the pilot it has to fit

`INSERT_PILOT_D` is **3.7 mm**. The M3 × 5.7 insert most suppliers stock (Ruthex, CNC
Kitchen) is **4.6 mm OD and wants a Ø4.0 hole** — it will not seat properly in 3.7 mm, and
on the Ø7.0 right-hand boss it leaves only 1.2 mm of wall to split. Buy an insert of
**OD ≤ 4.2 mm** — the M3 × 5 × 4.0 sizes in the common assortment kits are exactly right —
or drill the eight pilots out to Ø4.0 and use the 4.6 mm part.

### The etherCON flange thread is unconfirmed

Drawing 20003890 dimensions the *panel* cutout as **≥Ø3.2** — clearance for M3 — and the
flange holes are drawn with a thread convention, which means screws pass through the end
plate and thread into the connector. On that reading, the end plate is 2.3 mm thick, so
**M3 × 6** gives ~3.7 mm engagement and is the right part.

**The drawing does not say so in words, so check before ordering six.** The boards have all
three etherCONs fitted — try an M3 screw in one flange hole. If it threads, order M3 × 6. If
it is a clearance hole, order M3 × 8 with nuts and check nut clearance against the
neighbouring connector first: at 28 mm pitch the flanges are only 3 mm apart.

## Assembly order

1. Heat-set eight M3 × 5 inserts, four into each end face — the insert press jig in
   [`../jig/`](../jig/README.md) holds the tube upright and puts each pilot under the tip.
2. Slide the board in from the front, rear edge first, until the three NE8FBH plates
   reach the end face.
3. Offer the front plate over the three connector bosses, run the six M3 panel screws
   into the connector flanges and the four M3 × 8 into the tube.
4. Drop the PLA LED insert into the LED rebate; a dab of CA at two corners holds it. Fit
   the rear plate with its four screws.
5. Press the switch cover into its pocket, key into the slot; it comes out with a small
   screwdriver in the notch at its front end.

## Print settings

`docs/ENCLOSURE.md` assumed 0.6 mm nozzle, 0.3 mm layer, ASA. What is built is **ABS-GF at
0.4 mm / 0.24 mm layers** on a Bambu Lab H2C, with 2.5 mm walls as 6 × 0.42 fully solid.
Bores are already modelled +0.4 mm on diameter for FDM hole shrink; **do not add the
compensation twice** (`xy_hole_compensation = 0`).

[`../bambu/`](../bambu/) turns the STLs into plates for the H2C:

```bash
<venv>/bin/python hardware/enclosure/bambu/orient_for_print.py --tube   # model -> print pose
hardware/enclosure/bambu/build.sh absgf-0.4-tube                        # plate + slice + prove
hardware/enclosure/bambu/build.sh absgf-0.4-tube frontplate             # the fit-check article
hardware/enclosure/bambu/build.sh absgf-0.4-tube rearplate              # the rear plate alone
hardware/enclosure/bambu/build.sh pla-0.4-tube white-x4                 # 4 LED inserts + 4 switch covers
hardware/enclosure/bambu/build.sh pla-0.4-tube switchcover              # one switch cover
hardware/enclosure/bambu/build.sh pla-0.4-jig jig                       # the heat-set insert jig
hardware/enclosure/bambu/build.sh absgf-0.4-tube-x4 tube-x4             # four bodies + ends, one bed
```

`build.sh` needs `CANARY_H2C_REF_3MF` pointing at a Bambu Studio-made H2C project; see
`make_project_config.py`. `orient_for_print.py --tube` stands the body on its **rear** end
(with the front down, the guide-tube fins would point the wrong way, through the SW201
slot), lays the end plates flat, and drops each part onto z = 0.

**ABS-GF is the one to print.** It is stiff, dimensionally stable and happy in the H2C's
enclosed chamber, and Bambu ships an H2C profile for it only at 0.4 — which is why 0.4 is
the nozzle to fit rather than the 0.6 the concept doc assumed. It needs a hardened nozzle:
the glass fill will chew a brass one. Both H2C nozzles are hardened steel already.

**PLA is for a prototype, not the product.** The board's only fixing is six M3 panel screws
pulling three etherCON bodies against the front end plate; PLA creeps under sustained
preload, so those joints will slacken. It is fine for a case that sits on a bench.

**Print a front plate alone first.** A single front plate is a short print, and it gives a
real part to lay a real NE8FBH against — checks 1–3 below, the three that can scrap a
four-and-a-half-hour print. Do that before committing to a plate.

### What the slicer did with it

`support_report.py`: 0.0 g of support. Every *Bridge* and *Overhang wall* it emitted is
one of: the window's and rebate's upper edges (10 and 13 mm bridges), the SW201 slot's
upper edge (13 mm), the Ø4 bores' ceilings inside the guide tubes, the blind ends of the
four lower insert pilots (Ø3.7), and internal bridging over infill. The first layer is the
end ring plus the rail and boss ends, ~1500 mm², with an 8 mm brim outside it; an
88.5 mm-tall ring of ABS-GF is where the chamber earns its keep.

### Why the deliverable is Studio's own re-export

`build.sh` does not ship the project it assembles. It builds one, slices it to prove the
gcode, then hands that project back to Studio with `--slice 0 --export-3mf` and ships
**what Studio writes**, pre-sliced. A config assembled by hand carries preset-system
leftovers, and round-tripping makes Studio normalise them away.

**The one that actually broke it was array shape.** The H2C keeps *three extruder variants
per filament* — `filament_extruder_variant` names them (`Direct Drive Standard`,
`… High Flow`, `… E3D High Flow`) — so even a single-filament project carries 3-entry
arrays for every per-variant setting. Overlaying a filament preset's own values assigns
its leaf arrays straight in and collapses 35 of them to length 1, while 7 siblings stay at
3 and `filament_extruder_variant` itself says 1. Studio answers **"Invalid configuration
file"** and loads nothing; the CLI slices it without a murmur. So the project decides array
length, the preset decides values, and `make_project_config.py` now fails the build if any
array is reshaped.

`include` was a second, separate leak: the flattened preset carries
`include: ["fdm_filament_template_direct_dual_e3d"]`, an instruction to Bambu's *preset*
system, meaningless in a *project* and absent from every real Studio project.
`verify_3mf.py` gates every build on both:

```bash
python3 hardware/enclosure/bambu/verify_3mf.py <file.3mf>
```

It checks that every build item resolves to an object carrying triangles, that no
preset-only key leaked in, that the per-variant filament arrays agree with
`filament_extruder_variant`, and that filament/nozzle identity is self-consistent. It fails
the plates that shipped and passes both Studio's own output and a known-good Studio project.

**Slicing is not proof a file opens, and neither is a round-trip through the CLI.** The CLI
reads, slices and re-exports projects the GUI refuses on load — it re-exported the broken
one without complaint. The only proof is opening the file in Studio and looking, which is
how the real error was finally read. Do that before shipping a plate.

### A second filament

`make_project_config.py` also carries a dual-filament path (support material on the H2C's
second nozzle), written for the superseded two-shell case, whose domed interior needed
support. The tube needs none. The traps that path works around — flush-array sizing, the
two nozzles' different printable areas, machine G-code held in `include` templates, and
per-plate filament maps — are documented in the comments of `make_project_config.py`,
`flatten_preset.py`, `inject_config.py` and `verify_3mf.py`.

## Before you print a batch

The rev A boards exist and PCBWay fitted all three etherCONs (see `docs/FAB.md` — it was
against the DNP, but it means the connectors are on the board and every number below is
now measurable with callipers instead of read off a drawing). **Measure these three before
committing to a batch; each one is a scrap print if it is wrong.**

1. **Connector axis height above the PCB top surface — nominally 12.5 mm.** This is the
   critical one. Drawing 20003890 dimensions 12,5 from the *plate's bottom edge* to the
   axis, and the model takes that plate bottom as resting on the board's top surface. If it
   actually stands off, every bore and every screw hole in the front end plate is out by
   that standoff. The Ø22 bore has 0.2 mm of radial slack over the boss and Neutrik
   specifies the cutout as **≥Ø22**, so the bore forgives a little — but the connector
   locates on its **two Ø3.2 panel screws**, and an M3 in a Ø3.4 hole has only 0.2 mm. Half
   a millimetre of error here and the screws will not start.
2. **The protrusion in front of the mounting plate.** The drawing still does not dimension
   it — the `3,45` in the front view is plate-bottom to pin-tip, not the front boss. The
   B-series sibling NC5FBV dimensions 3.5 mm and both take the identical panel cutout, so
   the 2.5 mm end plates assume they match. If the boss is shorter than 2.5 mm the end
   plate has to be thinned locally at the bores.
3. **Latch-post midpoint to the plate's rear face — nominally 8.255 mm** (drawing: 3,81 to
   the first latch post, 8,89 between them). This is what puts the connector's mounting plate on the end plate's
   inner face so the six M3 panel screws can do their job as the board's primary fixing.
   The generator now asserts it: the board lands **0.25 mm forward of centre in its
   channel, with 0.50 mm available**, so it self-adjusts — but only while that 8.255 holds.

4. **28 mm etherCON pitch leaves 3 mm between adjacent front plates**, fixed by the routed
   board. Enough for the PUSH latch tabs, but check plug ergonomics on a first article.
5. **Shielding.** Leaving the R70's aluminium extrusion behind gives up a shield, and this
   board has a 25 MHz crystal, an SPI bus and an isolated CAN stage. Unlike the D1/D2
   concepts there is now no aluminium front plate to recover part of it, so this is a
   fully plastic box — decide the answer before pre-compliance.
   Note the flip side: `U301`'s PCB antenna simply works in a printed body.
6. **Lessons already folded in.** A first article of the earlier two-shell case (printed
   2026-09-10) warped, its screw posts held the halves apart, the ESP32 antenna fouled the
