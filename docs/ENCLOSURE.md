# dbCANary — enclosure

`DECISIONS.md` lists the enclosure as unchosen, with one note: *"The R70 is 115×110×35 mm extruded
aluminium; etherCON jacks are deeper than plain RJ45 and will drive the depth."* Both halves of that
turn out to be right, and the second one drives everything below.

This document proposes a 3D-printed body with laser-cut IO plates, sized to sit next to an R70
without looking like a different class of object.

> **This is the concept study. The buildable model is documented in
> [`hardware/enclosure/3dp/`](../hardware/enclosure/3dp/README.md), generated by
> `hardware/enclosure/gen_case_3dp.py --tube`** — a fully printed one-piece tube with
> screwed-on end plates, rather than D1/D2's tray-plus-plates. It resolves open items 1, 2 and 6 below, and drops the
> aluminium front plate, which changes the shielding answer (item 4).

## Correction: the board is 100 × 81 mm

`LAYOUT.md` says 92 × 72 mm. The board file says otherwise:

```
Edge.Cuts:  X 20 → 120   =  100 mm
            Y 19 → 100   =   81 mm
```

`LAYOUT.md` is stale — it also quotes 26 mm etherCON centres where the placed footprints are on 28 mm.
The rev A layout is finished and routed, so **100 × 81 mm and 28 mm centres are the numbers**, and the
doc should be corrected rather than the case designed to it. *(`LAYOUT.md` now carries a correction
note to this effect.)*

## Measured from the board file

Everything the enclosure has to serve, taken from placed footprints (board coordinates, and relative
to the board's own bottom-left corner at 20, 19):

| Ref | Part | Board xy | Rel xy | Exits which face |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `J201` `J202` `J401` | etherCON NE8FBH | 36, 64, 92 @ y=92 | 16, 44, 72 @ 73 | **front** (long edge, y=100) |
| `J102` | USB-C | 106, 25 | 86, 6 | **rear** (long edge, y=19) |
| `U301` | ESP32-S3-WROOM-1 | 54, 26.5 | 34, 7.5 | none — **antenna, rear edge** |
| `D301`–`D304` | 0603 status LEDs | 23.5 @ y=26…38 | 3.5 @ 7…19 | **top** (0603, emits +Z) |
| `SW301` `SW302` | PTS645 tact | 28 @ y=48, 58 | 8 @ 29, 39 | **top** (actuates +Z) |
| `SW201` | DIP slide, CAN terminator | 30, 76.5 | 10, 57.5 | **top** — must stay field-reachable (rule 6) |

Two consequences worth pulling out early:

- **The LEDs and both buttons are on the top face, not the left edge.** `LAYOUT.md` put the UI block
  "left edge, where a panel can see them", but they are 0603 parts emitting in +Z and a tact
  actuating in +Z. A left side panel sees nothing. The top plate is the UI surface.
- **The etherCON cluster is 6 mm left of board centre.** Cluster centre is at rel x = 44; board
  centre is 50. Left uncorrected, the finished box is visibly lopsided. Fixed below by offsetting the
  board inside the case rather than by moving anything on a board that is already fabricated.

## NE8FBH, from the vendor drawing

From `hardware/lib/ne8fbh.pdf` (Neutrik 20003890), which is the authority for every number here:

| | |
|---|---|
| Front plate | **25.0 W × 25.6 H mm** |
| Panel cutout bore | **Ø22.0 mm** |
| Panel screws | **2 × Ø3.2**, diagonally opposite, at **±9.9, ±9.9** from bore centre |
| Depth behind plate | **21.6 mm**, plus 2.7 mm rear tab |
| Axis height above PCB top | **12.5 mm** |

Note this is the **B-series** pattern — Ø22 bore with diagonal screws — not the D-series Ø23.80 with
screws at ±12.00 that nanODE uses. They are not interchangeable and the screws here clear the bore by
1.4 mm rather than notching into it.

At 28 mm pitch against a 25 mm plate there is **3 mm between adjacent front plates**. That is enough
for the PUSH latch tabs, which sit at the top centre of each plate, but it is tight and it is fixed
by the fabricated board.

## Envelope

The etherCON sets the height, exactly as `DECISIONS.md` predicted: a 25.6 mm front plate standing on
the board plane, plus whatever the board sits on, plus walls.

```
                                                         z (from outside bottom)
top plate ──────────────────────────────────────────────  36.0
   │  2.5 mm laser acrylic — UI surface, RF-transparent
   ├──────────────────────────────────────────────────── 33.5
   │  1.8 mm clearance
etherCON front plate top ─────────────────────────────── 31.7
   │
   │  NE8FBH front plate, 25.6 mm            axis @ 18.6
   │
board top ──────────────────────────────────────────────  6.1
   │  PCB 1.6
   ├────────────────────────────────────────────────────  4.5
   │  2.0 mm bosses
inner floor ────────────────────────────────────────────  2.5
   │  2.5 mm floor
                                                           0
```

| | Value | vs R70 |
|---|---|---|
| Internal | **118 × 87 × 31 mm** | |
| External | **123 × 92 × 36 mm** | 115 × 110 × 35 |
| Wall | 2.5 mm | |

Within a couple of millimetres on width and height; 18 mm shallower. If visual parity matters, the
depth pads out to ~110 mm with a rear cable-relief lip or side mounting flanges — but shallower is
better in an amp rack and there is no functional reason to add it.

**The 118 mm internal width is deliberate.** The board is 100 mm, so it sits with **15 mm of clear
space on the left and 3 mm on the right**, which puts the etherCON cluster centre exactly on the case
centreline. The 15 mm strip is not waste: it lands next to the LED column and makes an obvious badge
or legend area.

## Concept D1 — printed tray + laser front + laser top

```
        ┌────── 2.5 mm acrylic top: LEDs, buttons, SW201 slot ──────┐
     ┌──┴───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┴──┐
     │                                                                 │
     │              MAIN PCB on 2 mm bosses                            │  USB-C ▶ rear
     │                                                                 │
     └──┬───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┬──┘
        │ ╔═══════ 2 mm alu front plate ═══════╗                    │
        │ ║   (○)      (○)      (○)   3× Ø22   ║                    │
        └─╚════════════════════════════════════╝────────────────────┘
```

One printed part (floor + three walls, front face left open), two cut plates.

- **The front face is a separate 2 mm aluminium plate, not printed.** It carries three connectors on
  a pitch and takes every cable load in the product. Printed, ASA's 0.5–0.8 % shrink walks the 56 mm
  cluster span by ~0.34 mm against a Ø22 bore with well under that in clearance — the three
  connectors cannot all seat square. Cut, the pitch is exact and the plate spreads cable load across
  the whole front wall instead of into three printed bores.
- **The top plate is 2.5 mm smoked acrylic**, and this deletes work: the four 0603 LEDs read straight
  through tinted acrylic, so there are **no light pipes to model, print or fit**. Buttons get Ø6.2
  bores; `SW201` gets a recessed slot wide enough for a fingernail and narrow enough not to snag a
  cable.
- **The top plate must not be metal.** `U301`'s antenna is on the rear edge directly beneath it.

| | |
|---|---|
| Printed volume | ~60 cm³, ~72 g ASA |
| Print time | ~5–6 h |
| Per 256 mm plate | 2 bodies |
| **Throughput** | **~3 h of printer per unit** |

## Concept D2 — printed frame + four plates *(recommended for batch)*

Same footprint, but the printed part loses its floor and becomes a three-sided frame; the bottom
becomes a fourth cut plate.

```
    laser acrylic top ═══════════════════════════════
   ┌────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
   │ printed ASA frame: left, right, rear walls      │   ← rear wall carries USB-C only
   │        + 4 corner posts drilled through         │
   └────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
    laser alu front  ╔════════════════════════════╗
    laser alu bottom ═════════════════════════════════
              4× M3 tie-bolts, top plate → bottom plate
```

- **~41 g and ~3 h printed, so ~1.8 h per unit** — a little under half of D1, and the bottom plate
  costs almost nothing on a laser that is already cutting the other two.
- **No heat-set inserts anywhere.** Four M3 bolts run top plate to bottom plate through printed
  corner posts. The plastic sees compression only; every tensile load is carried in steel. This
  matters more than the time saving: a screw threaded into the top of an FDM wall is pulling directly
  against layer adhesion, and it is the standard way printed enclosures fail.
- The printed part now carries **no connector pitch at all** — three of four faces are cut plates,
  and the one printed wall has a single USB-C cutout on it.
- An aluminium bottom plate also gives back a little of the shielding lost by leaving the R70's
  extrusion behind, and a flat metal face to bolt to a rack shelf.

Costs: four cut parts instead of two, a seam on all four edges, and the frame is floppy until
assembled — fine in service, mildly annoying on the bench.

**Build D1 for the bring-up units going onto the rev A boards. Build D2 if more than a handful
follow.**

## Front plate geometry

Directly cuttable, all numbers derived above. Origin bottom-left of the plate.

| Feature | Position | Size |
|---|---|---|
| Plate | — | **123 × 36 × 2 mm**, 5052 or 6061 aluminium |
| Bore ×3 | x = **33.5, 61.5, 89.5**; y = **18.6** | Ø22.0 |
| Screws ×6 | bore centre **+(9.9, 9.9)** and **−(9.9, 9.9)** | Ø3.2 |
| Fixing ×4 | 6 mm in from each corner | M3 countersunk |

Margins check out: 22.5 mm from each end bore to the plate edge, 5.9 mm from the topmost screw to the
top edge, 6.0 mm between adjacent bore edges, and the diagonal screws of adjacent connectors sit at
different heights so they never clash. The bore centre at y = 18.6 on a 36 mm plate is within 0.6 mm
of centred, which is a coincidence but a welcome one.

If only a CO₂ laser is available it will not cut aluminium — either have the plates made externally,
or fall back to 3 mm acrylic and accept that acrylic under repeated etherCON cable yank is a wear
item.

## Process: FDM or resin

**FDM, for both concepts.** The one argument that usually gets skipped is worth stating first,
because it runs the other way:

**MSLA print time scales with part height, not plate area.** A 36 mm tall body at 0.05 mm layers is
720 layers at ~2.2 s ≈ **26 minutes**, whether the plate holds one part or six. Against FDM's 5–6 h
for the D1 tray, resin wins outright on machine time. Any comparison that omits this is wrong.

It still loses here, on four grounds that apply specifically to this part:

1. **Plate area.** A Saturn-class MSLA is 218 × 123 mm. The D1 body at 123 × 92 mm fits **one per
   plate**. The property that makes resin cheap in batch — a full plate costing the same as one part
   — is exactly what a part this size cannot use.
2. **Large thin-walled resin parts are the hard case.** A 123 × 92 mm footprint with 2.5 mm walls
   generates high per-layer peel force, cups against the FEP and warps during post-cure. This is a
   yield problem, not a time problem, and it is why big flat resin parts are rare.
3. **Notch sensitivity.** Corner posts, tie-bolt holes, the `SW201` slot, the rim of every cutout.
   Tough resins narrow the gap to ASA but do not close it, and they creep under sustained bolt
   preload where ASA does not.
4. **Per-unit labour.** Wash, cure, support removal, IPA. On a batch this dominates the hands-on cost
   and never amortises.

Plus HDT: tough resins land at 45–80 °C against ASA's ~95 °C. In a d&b amp rack that margin is not
academic.

**Where resin does earn its place:** small, detailed, high-multiple parts where the plate actually
fills — button caps for `SW301`/`SW302`, and a printed badge for the 15 mm legend strip. Not the
body, and not the top plate (clear resin yellows under UV faster than anything else available; use
acrylic).

## Material

| Material | Verdict |
|---|---|
| PLA | **No.** HDT ~55 °C and it creeps under bolt preload. Fit-check prototypes only. |
| PETG | Defensible here — this box lives indoors in a rack, not on a truss in sun. Cheap and tough-ish, but it creeps and bolted joints relax over time. |
| **ASA** | **Primary.** HDT ~95 °C, UV-stable, good impact, matte black straight off the spool, hides layer lines. Needs an enclosed printer. |
| ABS | Same class, cheaper, not UV-stable. ASA is ABS plus UV for very little more. |
| PC / PC-ABS | HDT 110–140 °C. Overkill unless a flammability or temperature spec demands it. |
| PET-CF | Very low warp and stiff — the upgrade if the D2 frame proves floppy in a long rack. |
| TPU 95A | A printed gasket in the top plate groove, if splash resistance is ever wanted. |

**ASA, black, textured plate for the show faces.** PETG is the honest budget answer for a rack-only
product and should not be dismissed — but a single material across both dbCANary and nanODE is worth
something in a workshop, and nanODE genuinely needs the UV stability.

If these ever ship rather than get built, look at a **UL94 V-0** filament for the body. Standard ASA
is HB. Nothing here is mains-adjacent — it is PoE and USB — so V-0 is good practice rather than a
requirement, but it is the sort of thing that is cheap to decide early and expensive to retrofit.

### Print settings that matter

| | |
|---|---|
| Nozzle | **0.6 mm** — 2.5 mm walls as 4 × 0.625, solid, no infill, ~2× faster than 0.4 |
| Layer | 0.3 mm |
| Chamber | enclosed and warm; ASA layer adhesion is the reason for choosing ASA |
| Walls | 2.5 mm structural, 4.0 mm at corner posts |
| Bore compensation | model cutouts **+0.4 mm** on diameter; FDM holes finish 0.3–0.5 mm undersize |
| Shrink | measure a 100 mm coupon per spool and scale per axis — then still keep every pitch on a cut plate |

## What leaving the aluminium extrusion costs

**Shielding — the real regression.** The R70's extrusion is a shield. This board has a 25 MHz
crystal, an SPI bus to the W5500, an isolated CAN stage and a switching front end, and it will
radiate more in plastic than in metal. Partial recovery: the aluminium front plate and (in D2) the
aluminium bottom plate are already tied to `CHASSIS` through the NE8FBH bodies, so they do useful
work for free. Full recovery would need nickel spray inside, with a deliberate unmasked window over
`U301`'s antenna. Worth settling before pre-compliance, not after.

**One consequence to keep an eye on.** A conductive front plate ties all three etherCON shells
together mechanically. Today that is already true electrically — `LAYOUT.md` rule 2 puts all three
shells on `CHASSIS`, single-point bonded to `GND`. But if the CAN-side shells are ever isolated to
respect the barrier, **the metal plate silently defeats it.** The isolation barrier is the one thing
in this design that must not acquire an accidental bridge, and a front plate is exactly the sort of
part that gets added without re-reading rule 1.

**Thermal — fine.** External surface is ~0.034 m², and the board dissipates a few watts:

```
ΔT ≈ 3 W / (0.034 m² × ~9 W/m²K)  ≈  10 K rise
```

About 10 K above ambient, against ASA's 95 °C HDT. No vents needed.

**RF — better, not worse.** `U301` is an ESP32-S3-WROOM-1 with a PCB antenna at the board edge, and
`LAYOUT.md` rule 3 already protects it with a copper keepout. In an aluminium extrusion that antenna
was going to be compromised no matter how carefully the board was laid out; in a printed body with an
acrylic top it simply works. WiFi is a fallback path in rev A, so this is not decisive — but it is a
point in plastic's favour rather than against.
